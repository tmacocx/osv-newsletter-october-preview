ONE QUESTION, ANSWERED
Send us yours: https://ourspecialvillagetn.com/contact

November's question: "Will using an AAC device stop my child from learning to talk?"
Almost every parent worries about this, and the evidence points the other way: children given a reliable way to communicate tend to speak more, not less. AAC is not only for kids with no words; many autistic kids lose language when they are dysregulated, and a device can carry them through.

1. Start without waiting. There is no readiness test and no age floor. A picture board on the fridge counts, and nobody has to approve it first.
2. Model, don’t quiz. Point to “more” as you say “more.” Then wait longer than feels polite; the answer at second eight still counts.
3. Keep it close. At dinner, in the car, at Grandma’s. Never take AAC away as a consequence: it is a voice, not a privilege.

What is AAC?: https://ourspecialvillagetn.com/resources/what-is-aac
Getting a device: https://ourspecialvillagetn.com/resources/aac-devices
Parent-to-parent guidance, not medical advice.
