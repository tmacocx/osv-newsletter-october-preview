February’s question

“How do I know if my child’s therapist is a good fit?”

Fit matters more than a long list of initials. A good match can explain what they are doing and why, and progress shows up outside the therapy room.

1

Ask what they do every week. What share of their caseload has needs like your child’s, and when do they refer out? Credentials are clues, not proof.

2

Watch how your child is treated. Green flags: your child’s communication, including AAC and “no,” is respected, and goals connect to real life.

3

Know you can switch. Repeated distress, loss of trust, or goals you did not agree to are valid reasons to ask for a plan review, a different clinician, or a second opinion.
