New guide · reviewed Sept 2026

Therapy styles: play, structure, and compliance

Two therapists with the same credentials can run completely different sessions. This guide shows you what to watch for: who directs the session, how structured it is, how your child’s communication is supported, and what happens when they say no. It comes with green flags and red flags you can use across speech, OT, PT, feeding, counseling, and ABA.

One question, answered

A real parent question, answered plainly. Parents are rarely told how much say they actually have.

 

Question

“What do I do if my child’s IEP isn’t being followed?”

Answer

An IEP is a legally binding plan: the school has to deliver every service and accommodation written in it, not just the ones that are convenient. When that is not happening, you have more options than most parents are told.

1

Put it in writing. Email the case manager and copy the principal: name the service or accommodation, quote what the IEP says, describe what is actually happening, and ask for a fix by a specific date. Ask for the service logs and progress data too; you are entitled to them. Keep the sent copy.

2

Ask for an IEP team meeting. You can request one in writing at any time, not just at the annual review, and the school has to respond. If things stall, copy the district’s special education director. Bring your notes and a friend to take notes for you.

3

Use the state’s free complaint process. If it still is not fixed, you can file an administrative complaint with the Tennessee Department of Education. No lawyer, no cost. “Failing to implement IEP accommodations or services” is one of the state’s own listed examples. TDOE must investigate and issue a written decision within 60 calendar days, and you have one year from the problem to file. Mediation is also available.

4

Get free backup. STEP TN, Tennessee’s parent training center, will review the IEP with you and help you prepare: 800-280-7837 (Español 800-975-2919). For a district that will not follow its own plan, Disability Rights Tennessee: 800-342-1660.

Bring your own version of this question to Village Hall on Sat Oct 10. Mercedes Lawson of A.C.C.E.S.S. is covering the IEP process and taking parent questions live.
