October in Our Special Village

Welcome to Our Special Village! I’m Dr. Taylor Hickok, a local speech-language pathologist, parent of an AuDHD child, and founder of this community.

I created Our Special Village after seeing how often families of neurodivergent children were left trying to navigate services, schools, therapies, and community resources on their own. Too many parents were asking the same questions without one clear place to find answers.

This month’s newsletter brings together local events, practical resources, and opportunities to connect with other families. I hope it saves you a little time – and reminds you that you do not have to figure everything out alone.

With love,

Wall of Hope

 

Ellie’s drawing win

Ellie is four years old and has always struggled with fine motor skills. Because of this, she has avoided drawing and attempting to write letters at all costs because she is a perfectionist and is so petrified of doing something poorly.

After lots of DIR Floortime work with her OT Alyssa that worked on prewriting, she picked up a marker, and during play drew x’s on a cardboard box. I had NEVER seen her draw more than 2 seconds of scribbles and this was an incredible win for her.

✎ Taylor, Murfreesboro

October’s question

“What do I do if my child’s IEP isn’t being followed?”

An IEP is legally binding. The school has to deliver what is written in it.

1

Put it in writing. Email the case manager and copy the principal: name what is missing, quote the IEP, ask for a fix by a date, and request service logs.

2

Ask for an IEP team meeting. Request one in writing any time. If things stall, copy the district special education director. Bring notes and a friend.

3

Use free state help. File a TDOE administrative complaint (no lawyer, decision in 60 days), or call STEP TN at 800-280-7837.
